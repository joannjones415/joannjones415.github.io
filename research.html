Disentangling constraints on S8 from the high and low redshift CMB

CMB measurements have information from both high and low redshift due to lensing, and tests of S8 traditionally conflate the two. We present a technique to extract constraints on the cosmological parameters from each redshift component separately, enabling a high-z vs. low-z clustering comparison that was not previously possible. We marginalize over lensing information using a principal component analysis, and show that the primordial CMB from combined Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 prefers S8 = 0.795 ± 0.022, in agreement with the recent determination from the Dark Energy Survey of S8 = 0.789 ± 0.012.

Advised by S. Dodelson, with U. de Andrade Uchoa · UChicago · 2025–present

Scalar field thawing dark energy constraints

I provide constraints on the simplest physically motivated models of evolving dark energy, in which acceleration is driven by a slowly rolling scalar field. Bayesian analysis using data from DES Y6 3×2pt, DESI DR2 BAO, the DES-Dovekie supernovae, and the CMB from Planck, ACT, and SPT. In prep.

Advised by J. Frieman · UChicago · 2026–present

Detectability of thawing dark energy in LSST supernova data

If dark energy is a scalar field with isocurvature perturbations, its fluctuating density could leave a small signal on the supernova distance–redshift relation. We forecast how well Vera C. Rubin Observatory LSST supernovae can measure this signal.

Advised by J. Frieman · UChicago · 2025–present

Uncorrelated anomalies in the CMB

Several summary statistics of CMB isotropy have anomalous values. Individually, their low p-values are weak evidence for violation of statistical isotropy. We study the tail values of these statistics and find very little correlation among them. We show that the joint probability of all four anomalies occurring by chance in ΛCDM is likely ≤ 3 × 10−8. Our results provide strong evidence for a violation of statistical isotropy.

Advised by G. D. Starkman and C. J. Copi, with Y. Akrami · CWRU · 2023–present

  • paperStrong evidence against a statistically isotropic universe, arXiv:2310.12859

Previous projects

Machine learning for relativistic electron precipitation

I developed an unsupervised machine learning method to find relativistic electron precipitation bands in years of SAMPEX electron count data. It combines power spectral density with a self-organizing map, a competitive neural network, to catalog the band events automatically.

Advised by S. Vidal-Luengo and L. Blum · LASP, CU Boulder · summer 2022

Macroscopic dark matter search

I tested machine learning methods for image classification in a citizen science project that connects the search for macroscopic dark matter with public outreach.

Advised by G. D. Starkman · CWRU · 2022–2023
