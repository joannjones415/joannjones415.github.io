Scalar field thawing dark energy constraints for DES Y6

In preparation. I constrain the simplest physically motivated models of dynamical dark energy, in which a slowly rolling scalar field drives cosmic acceleration, with DES Y6 3×2pt, DESI DR2 BAO, the DES-Dovekie supernovae, and CMB data from Planck, ACT, and SPT.

Detectability of thawing dark energy in LSST supernova data

Thawing dark energy can carry isocurvature perturbations, so it varies across the sky as well as in time. I forecast how well Vera C. Rubin Observatory LSST supernovae can constrain this evolving, spatially varying dark energy.

Advised by J. Frieman · UChicago · 2024–present

Disentangling constraints on S8 from the high and low redshift CMB

The CMB measures clustering at two epochs: at recombination through the primary anisotropies, and near z ≈ 2 through lensing. A single CMB value of S8 mixes the two. I marginalize over lensing with a few principal components of the lensing power spectrum, so that Planck, ACT, and SPT give S8 = 0.795 ± 0.022 from recombination alone, in agreement with DES Y6. The clustering inferred from CMB lensing is larger, by an amount that depends on the prior on the neutrino masses.

Advised by S. Dodelson, with U. de Andrade Uchoa · UChicago · 2025–present

  • paperDisentangling constraints on S8 from the high and low redshift CMB, arXiv:2610.37752

Uncorrelated anomalies in the CMB

The CMB shows several anomalies on large angular scales. Using simulated ΛCDM skies, I showed that they are close to uncorrelated with one another, so their probabilities multiply. Together they give a p-value of about 3 × 10−8, more than a 5σ departure from ΛCDM.

Advised by G. D. Starkman and C. J. Copi, with Y. Akrami · CWRU · 2023–present

  • paperStrong evidence against a statistically isotropic universe, arXiv:2310.12859

Previous projects

Machine learning for relativistic electron precipitation

I developed an unsupervised machine learning method to find relativistic electron precipitation bands in years of SAMPEX electron count data. It combines power spectral density with a self-organizing map, a competitive neural network, to catalog the band events automatically.

Advised by S. Vidal-Luengo and L. Blum · LASP, CU Boulder · summer 2022

Macroscopic dark matter search

I tested machine learning methods for image classification in a citizen science project that connects the search for macroscopic dark matter with public outreach.

Advised by G. D. Starkman · CWRU · 2022–2023

cmb-anomaly-correlations

Computes four large-angle anomaly statistics on the Planck 2018 temperature maps and on ΛCDM realizations, and turns them into individual and joint p-values. It reproduces the analysis in arXiv:2310.12859.
